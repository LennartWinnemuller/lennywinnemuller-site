title: French politics (bond yields are the price of trust)
date: 2026-10-24
description: France is borrowing at its highest rate in 24 years, and both
  leading election camps promise more than the numbers allow. Here’s what the
  bond market is really telling us, and why rising government yields matter for
  every long-term investor.
---
French debt hit a record, 119% of GDP, and projected 122% next year. A deficit of 5.4% and 10-year bond yields at a 24-year high of 5%. 

Debt is rising, and so is the cost of financing it.

Simultaneously, the economy is barely growing, and the population is increasingly aging,

Borrowing above 5% while growing under 1%,  and its aging population both suppresses growth (fewer workers) and inflates spending (more pensioners), while making the only real fix, pension reform, politically impossible.

…. Meanwhile in the country we have seen violent riots, because parts of the population are actually demanding extra spending…..

We can see a big problem.

Next year elections with two major candidates (Le Pen, far right RN) 37% and Mélenchon (hard left, LFI) 18%.

What are both saying as a solution:

Le Pen is saying she will have drastic budget cuts, but at the same time she is proposing tax cuts and a lower retirement age for some.

Very contradictory, how do you drastically cut the budget while lowering taxes?

Their proposed answer is:  less EU spending, less spending on immigrants, cracking down on fraud.

The political strategy is : the bond market read the austerity, so it does not keeps demanding a higher rate (making the debt more expensive), and the voters read tax cuts and lower retirement age. 

Bond market is not buying it though, and keep demanding a higher yield (remember the 24 year high).

Mélenchon says he is  going to freeze certain parts of the debt.

In other words don’t pay interest on it anymore. Subsequently use the freed-up capacity for public spending/investment.

Well, if that would happen:

1. Markets would only lend to France at exorbitant rates (as they just decide not to pay interest anymore on their existing debt) meaning France can’t close its budget deficit. 

2. The French financial sector will have a huge problem as they use French bonds as collateral for their day-to-day funding, so the freeze hits their solvency. French government would need to rescue their banks, but the government won’t have any money to finance that.

3. There would be spillover effects as markets would fear other countries could do the same. 

So why does he propose something impossible:

1. His opponents do the marketing. Every warning from Europe “proves his point”, that the system is against the regular French citizen that is suffering. His voters don’t own French bonds, so the fact that Europe and Germany  is against it makes the proposal more credible.

2.  Debt (and its consequences) is the main issue for the median voter in the election, if he says the opposite of what Le Pen says, it makes him relevant.

3. It’s a starting point of negotiation around the debt with Europe.

**Conclusion**

Zoom out, and we see the same menu from candidates:

Immediate, visible benefits for your voters and costs pushed onto an entity your voters don't identify with

And those rising yields reprice everything, the cost of lost trust never stays with the bondholders.

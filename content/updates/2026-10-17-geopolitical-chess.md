## Iran and the Strait of Hormuz

Iran is attempting to pressure the United States by disrupting shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, pushing global oil prices higher and adding to inflation and bond yields.

By threatening Gulf countries, Iran is also seeking to increase the regional cost of continued US military pressure, and to encourage America’s regional allies to push Washington towards de-escalation.

## The United States

The United States, in turn, is attempting to hurt Iran economically by severely restricting its oil exports, while seeking alternative routes and sources of supply to reduce the disruption to Gulf energy exports.

The US economy appears somewhat more resilient, although the inflation consequences of a prolonged energy shock remain uncertain.

## Europe: a second energy shock

Europe seems to pay a higher price through weaker growth, higher energy costs and inflationary pressure. It is Europe’s second energy shock in a few years.

France is particularly vulnerable. High public debt, a substantial budget deficit, very weak economic growth and strong resistance to austerity create a difficult outlook. Higher bond yields compound these problems by increasing refinancing costs, making it all more painful.

## Russia

Meanwhile, Trump might look for Putin’s cooperation to relieve the economic pressure. Russia is getting hammered by attacks on its refineries, while benefiting from an elevated oil price. Putin would welcome Trump’s help with stopping the attacks on its refineries.

## My view

Washington has the stronger overall hand in terms of economic, military and energy resources. Iran’s strategy is to make the cost of continued confrontation increasingly painful for the US and its allies.

I think the US has the better watch, but Iran has more time.
